wk_commit_dtype LANDED: the reader nine markers said was missing, gated, with one named divergence

`uop/weak.bend` gains `wk_commit_dtype` and its `Bnd` -> `Maybe<I64>` helper, and
`.agents/slop/wk-cd-gate.sh` gates them: 7 rows, three lanes, 6 byte-identical, 1 documented
divergence.

THE MARKERS WERE NOT WRONG AND THEIR LABEL WAS. Nine of them read "`commit_dtype` is not
ported". Both halves of that were wrong in a way worth separating:

  THE ARITHMETIC WAS ALREADY THERE. `D.commit_dtype` (`mixin/dtype.bend:283`) is CPython's
  `commit_int` under a weakint test and `strong_dtype` otherwise, and it takes the node's
  dtype and its two bounds ALREADY RESOLVED -- which is CPython's own signature, so the
  port's is not a workaround for anything.

  THE BOUNDS WERE ALREADY THERE. `UOp.vmin` / `UOp.vmax` (`uop/fold.bend:4708`) are ported
  over the sweep's `BTable`, and the `_min_max` arithmetic core is at `fold.bend:2648`. The
  note at the top of weak.bend still called the sweep the biggest piece left in that half of
  ops.py and listed `i64_mul` / `i64_div` / `i64_mod` as missing; all three now exist.

  WHAT WAS MISSING WAS ONE CALL, and it could not be written where the arithmetic lives.
  `mixin/dtype.bend` does not import `uop/weak.bend`, and `wk_dt` is here -- so the reader
  had to go HERE and import dtype.bend. MEASURED, that edge closes NO cycle: `ops.bend`,
  `fold.bend` and `spec.bend` all do not import `weak.bend`. **So the wall nine markers
  describe was an IMPORT TOPOLOGY, not a language limit** -- and a wall gets written as a
  property of the language and read as one.

THE GATE HAS TO CONTAIN TWO ROW-SETS OR IT PROVES NOTHING, and both were found by writing it:

  THE FIXTURE MUST BE A weakint PARAM. A PARAM whose dtype is int32 answers `dtypes.i32` on
  EVERY row, because the weakint branch is not taken and `commit_dtype` returns
  `self.dtype`. The first oracle had seven rows of `i32`.

  THE ROWS THAT DISCRIMINATE ARE THE BIG ONES. `commit_int`'s ladder is
  `(default_int, dtypes.int, dtypes.long, ...)`, so with `default_int = int32` its FIRST
  rung holds every range inside int32 -- `cd_0_10`, `cd_neg5_5` and `cd_i32full` all answer
  i32 and say nothing. `cd_big_2p40` ([-2**40, 2**40]) and `cd_one_2p40` ([2**40, 2**40], a
  POINT range too wide for int32, which also reaches `commit_int`'s `lo == hi` arm) fall
  through to int64. A control that made the reader ignore its bounds and answer a fixed
  [0, 0] turns this gate RED, so the rows are load-bearing.

ONE DOCUMENTED DIVERGENCE, `cd_none`, PINNED ON BOTH SIDES. CPython answers `i64`; the port
answers nothing, because a PARAM with no `vmin_vmax` gives the bounds sweep no interval to
report. `wk_commit_dtype` is FAITHFUL here -- it hands `D.commit_dtype` exactly what
`UOp.vmin`/`UOp.vmax` gave it -- so the gap is the sweep's PARAM-with-no-bounds arm and not
the reader. Both sides' content is asserted, so it cannot change unnoticed in either
direction, and the message on the port side says what to do if the sweep arm is ever fixed.

A LIFETIME FRICTION WORTH RECORDING, because the tree already answered it. `D.commit_dtype`
answers `Maybe<&1, S.Dt>` and `F.dt_str` takes `Maybe<&2, S.Dt>`, and there is no `retag`
helper. The port's own rows MATCH the `Maybe` and hand the inner value to the printer, which
rebinds at whatever lifetime the scope wants -- so the gate does that, and a retag def would
have been the wrong answer to a question already answered three times in this tree.

AND THE BLOCK NOTE IS CORRECTED, so the next reader is not sent after a sweep that is already
landed: the nine markers are now blocked on their own RULE BODIES -- `absorb_weak_src`,
`lower_weak_node`, `pm_lower_weak` rules 0-2, `cast_consts`, `pm_cast_const` -- and not on a
reader.
