READ THIS BEFORE QUOTING IT. The sweep is exhaustive over `d < 2**22` and the
reachable domain is `d <= vmax`, which at int32/uint32 is `d < 2**32` -- so the
TOP of the domain is NOT covered and `s` could still climb there.  What the
sweep does establish is that `s` PEAKS at a `d` near `2**15` (the argmax column)
and then FALLS, so the wide-`d` tail is not where a larger `s` lives; and the
analytic bound `s <= 2*nbits` is what covers the uncovered tail.

THE ANSWER TO THE WALL'S OWN QUESTION, at the 32-bit dtypes the wall names:
  `magicgu` at int32/uint32 needs `2**s` of at most 48/49 bits MEASURED, and
  at most 62/64 bits BY THE BOUND.  49 bits FITS A SIGNED 64-BIT PAIR, so an
  `i64_shl` answers `magicgu`'s `2**s` at 32 bits with 14 bits to spare and NO
  65-bit carrier is needed there.  The 64-BIT dtypes are a different wall: they
  measured s = 80, i.e. 81 bits, which `i64_shl` cannot help with at all.
